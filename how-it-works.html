How It Works

This page walks through the steps that notes would go through, from the moment they are added to the tool to the moment they show up as something someone needs to do that day.

Step 1: Add Meeting Notes

The first step would be adding the notes or transcript from a meeting. This could be typed notes, something copied from a group chat, or a transcript from a recorded call. The person wouldn't have to clean the notes up first or rewrite them in any special format. The whole point is that messy, normal meeting notes should still work.

Step 2: Find Action Items

Once the notes are added, the system would go through them looking for the parts that actually matter, like who is doing something, what decision was made, or what still needs to be figured out. A lot of a meeting is just talking things through, so this step is really about separating the important action items from the rest of the conversation.

Step 3: Organize the Work

After the action items are found, they would be organized into actual tasks. Each task could be connected to a project, given to a specific person, and given a due date if one was mentioned in the meeting. This is the step where the notes stop being a wall of text and start looking more like a normal task list.

Step 4: Break Down Larger Tasks

Some tasks mentioned in a meeting are too big to just check off in one sitting, like "finish the prototype" or "get ready for the presentation." For tasks like that, the system could break them into smaller steps so the task feels more manageable and it's more obvious what to actually start on first.

Step 5: Know What To Do Today

The last step would combine the most important tasks from all of someone's different projects into one daily checklist. Instead of checking several different meeting notes or project pages, a person could just open their checklist and see what actually needs their attention that day.
